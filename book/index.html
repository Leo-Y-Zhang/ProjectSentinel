The four conclusions that shape our advice

  1. The window is open now and closes on its own

    Three of the six credible independent comparables have been acquired in twenty-four months. Sentinel is the last sizeable independent asset in regulatory reporting and trade surveillance, and scarcity is the single most valuable attribute the Company currently owns. It is not a durable one.

  2. This is a strategics-only process — sponsors cannot reach the price

    At $76.00 a financial sponsor underwrites to roughly a 10% IRR, against a 20%+ hurdle. Sponsors only clear their hurdle at approximately today's trading price, where the Board would have no reason to transact. We recommend against a broad auction: it would import price-insensitive bidders, raise leak risk with regulated customers, and depress rather than improve outcome.

  3. Buyers will be dilutive on reported EPS, and that must be managed, not hidden

    Our modelled transaction is 7.3% dilutive to GAAP EPS in the first full year, reaching approximate neutrality by year three. Every credible buyer will run this analysis. Our job is to arrive with the answer already framed — because, as page 11 shows, most of that dilution is not caused by the premium at all.

  4. Value is defended on cash earnings and synergy capacity, not headline multiple

    At 25.2× headline the asset looks expensive. At 16.0× post-synergy it is squarely inside the precedent range. The bidders who can underwrite the second number are the bidders who can pay, which is why buyer selection and synergy diligence are the two workstreams that determine price.

Recommended next step

Authorise preparation of confidential materials and a management presentation, with first-round outreach to Tier 1 buyers in the week of 14 September. We would not approach Tier 2 or sponsors until first-round indications are in hand.

Why this transaction happens now

Page 4 · The story

A sale is not compelling because the Company is troubled. It is compelling because four independent clocks are running at once, and they will not stay aligned.

  1. The regulatory supercycle that built the Company is annualising

    The 2023–25 rulemaking wave — consolidated audit trail expansion, off-channel communications enforcement, and the T+1 settlement transition — drove three years of 20%+ growth. Those mandates are now largely implemented. Management's own plan guides FY27 organic growth to 11%, and the deceleration is arithmetic, not execution. A business is worth most when it is growing into a mandate, not after it has delivered one.

  2. Customers are consolidating and want one vendor, not three

    Regional bank and mid-market broker-dealer consolidation is removing roughly 4% of the addressable account base annually, and survivors are rationalising vendors. Buyers increasingly tender surveillance, regulatory reporting and communications archiving as a single lot. Sentinel is excellent at two of the three legs and absent from the third. Every quarter that persists, the Company is more likely to be a component of someone's bid than the recipient of one.

  3. The re-platforming bill is due, and the Company cannot fund it invisibly

    Surveillance is being rebuilt away from lexicon-and-rules toward language models that read intent. We estimate $180m over three years to remain competitive. Funded internally, that compresses EBITDA margin from 32% toward the mid-20s and takes the Company below the Rule-of-40 threshold on which its multiple depends. Inside a larger acquirer the same spend is absorbed against a far bigger revenue base and is very nearly invisible.

  4. The register wants an answer

    Two pre-IPO sponsors hold 22% between them, in funds now in years nine and eleven. Lock-ups expired long ago and both have signalled they will support a transaction. A register with two structurally motivated sellers is a register that eventually produces a discounted secondary block instead of a control premium.

  5. Buyer capacity is unusually good, and that is the part outside our control

    Strategic balance sheets are strong, investment-grade financing is open at rates that support this structure, and at least one likely bidder is under public activist pressure to deploy capital rather than accumulate it. Financing windows and activist campaigns both close without notice.

  6. Scarcity has already been demonstrated by others

    The acquisitions of three comparable assets since 2024 established both a price band and a strategic template. That is useful to us: precedent does the arguing. It also means the remaining buyers know exactly what they missed, which is the most productive condition in which to run a process.

Source: Company management; Haldane & Co. industry analysis. Addressable-account attrition based on announced regional bank consolidations.

Why the sponsor case fails

  • At 25.2× EBITDA, debt capacity of 6.5× funds barely a quarter of the purchase price — the equity cheque exceeds $3.1bn.
  • The asset is a 32%-margin software business, so there is no operational turnaround to underwrite; a sponsor is buying the same plan management already owns.
  • Multiple expansion cannot be assumed on exit from an entry already above the precedent median.
  • The $180m re-platforming spend lands inside the hold period and depresses exit-year EBITDA precisely when it is being capitalised.

What this means for process design

  • Include two sponsors in round one for price discovery and optionality — not because they will win.
  • Do not build the timetable around sponsor diligence needs; strategics move faster and the calendar should reflect that.
  • Resist any pressure to broaden the sponsor list. Additional financial bidders add leak risk without adding price.
  • If no strategic engages above $70, the honest recommendation is to stand down rather than transact — a failed process at a low clearing price is worse than no process.

The mechanism, and why it matters in negotiation

Identifiable intangibles are fair-valued independently of what is paid. Developed technology is worth what it is worth; customer relationships are worth what they are worth. Purchase price affects goodwill, which is not amortised. So the $104.7m annual amortisation charge is the same at $58 as it is at $82 — it is a consequence of buying a software company at all, not of buying this one at a premium.

At our recommended price, 62% of the first-year GAAP dilution exists before a single dollar of premium is paid. A buyer who anchors on reported EPS is therefore negotiating against its own accounting policy rather than against us. The correct frame — and the one every recent acquirer in this sector has adopted publicly — is cash EPS, on which this transaction is broadly neutral in year one and clearly accretive thereafter.

How we would use this

  • In first-round guidance: pre-empt it. Provide the purchase price allocation early so bidders model it correctly rather than conservatively.
  • Against a lowball: a bidder claiming EPS dilution as the reason for a $66 indication is describing a 5.8% problem it would still have at 4.5% for free.
  • With our own Board: expect the winning bidder's announcement to lead on cash EPS and synergies. That is not spin; it is the correct measure, and we should not be surprised by it.
Source: Haldane & Co. analysis. "Attributable to premium" is the incremental dilution relative to a transaction executed at the unaffected price on identical terms.

Consideration mix — more stock reduces dilution

Counterintuitive, and worth stating plainly to the Board: Aegis's earnings yield is 2.4% (FY26E) while its blended after-tax cost of new debt is 4.5%. Its equity is therefore the cheaper currency, and a cash-heavy structure is the more dilutive one.

Why we still recommend 50/50 rather than the EPS-optimal 75%

Our two pre-IPO sponsors hold 22% and need liquidity, not paper. A stock-heavy structure hands them a disposal problem and invites an overhang that damages the very currency being issued. The 50/50 structure also gives the buyer a defensible deleveraging narrative — 2.64× falling to 1.96× within three years — which protects its rating and therefore its willingness to pay.

Process design, risks and timetable

Page 14

Recommended process architecture

  • Targeted, two-round process. Four Tier 1 strategics plus two sponsors for price discovery. No broad auction.
  • No public announcement of a review. Sentinel's customers are regulated entities that consider vendor viability at renewal; a public process is a live commercial risk during the selling season.
  • Staggered disclosure. Cohort retention data and the re-platforming plan release only at second round, under a clean team where a bidder competes directly.
  • Pre-cleared purchase price allocation. Provide the intangibles analysis early so bidders do not model dilution conservatively and discount their price accordingly.
